RobotArena ∞

Unlimited robot benchmarking via real-to-sim translation — ICLR 2026

Evaluating a robot policy honestly is expensive. Real-world evaluation does not scale, and hand-authored simulation benchmarks cover a narrow, hand-picked slice of the world. RobotArena ∞ takes a third route: turn the robot video that already exists — millions of teleoperation episodes sitting in public datasets — into physics-consistent simulated environments you can evaluate in, perturb, and re-run without limit.

The work was done at Carnegie Mellon University with Prof. Katerina Fragkiadaki, and was accepted to ICLR 2026 (Jangir et al., 2026).

My contributions

Real-to-sim pipeline. I built the path from a teleoperation video to a simulated environment you can actually run a policy in: generative scene modeling to recover 3D geometry and appearance, and differentiable rendering to make the reconstruction agree with the observed pixels. The output is not a pretty scene — it is a physics-consistent one, with object poses and contacts that hold up when a policy starts interacting with them.

Automatic camera–robot calibration. Calibration is the quiet blocker in real-to-sim: if the camera-to-robot transform is wrong, everything downstream is wrong, and the standard fix is a human with a checkerboard. I designed a calibration method built on 3D Gaussian Splatting that recovers the transform with sub-centimeter pose error and no manual supervision. Running it over BridgeV2, DROID and RH20T produced more than 10,000 calibrated trajectories — the raw material the rest of the pipeline consumes.

Real teleoperation video is translated into a simulated counterpart that policies can be evaluated in at scale.

    Abs HTML

    RobotArena infinity shifts vision-language-action policy evaluation into large-scale simulated environments built automatically from real robot video. The pipeline converts demonstrations from widely used robot datasets into physics-consistent simulated counterparts using vision-language models, 2D-to-3D generative modeling and differentiable rendering, requiring only a single RGB image from a static-camera demonstration. Policies are scored by both VLM-guided automatic evaluation and scalable human preference judgments.
